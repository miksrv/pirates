# Map Generation

Code: `shared/game/map.ts`, `shared/game/islandShape.ts` · Render: `client/src/phaser/views/obstacleView.ts` (`createIslandView`) · Tuning: `MAP_ISLAND_COUNT`, `MAP_ROCK_COUNT`, `MAP_TILE_SIZE` in `shared/game/constants.ts`

## World
- No world-wide tile grid — continuous coordinates over one big water `TileSprite` (`water_fill_1`, `GROUND_TILE_KEY`). Islands rasterize their own local tile grid (below).
- Obstacles (islands, reefs, drift barrels, rocky shores) are placed by rejection sampling, not a grid/noise algorithm.

## Islands
- Shape: 5–10 randomly angled/sized lobes (overlapping ellipses) around a center, plus overall X/Y stretch — see `generateIslandShape`.
- Same shape recipe drives the visible sand/grass grid and the physics collision — collision uses the rasterized tile grid (`generateCollisionTiles`), so a ship collides with exactly the tiles it sees, not an approximate circle cluster.
- Placement: `tryPlaceObstacle` rejects islands too close to the ship spawn (`SAFE_ZONE_RADIUS`) or overlapping existing obstacles.
- **Shallow-water slowdown**: ships on shallow-water tiles (the ring around the coastline) get a `shallowWater` effect — `MAP_SHALLOW_SPEED_MULT` (0.5×) speed. The effect auto-expires when the ship leaves. Icon: 🏖️.

## Coastline layers (rendered back-to-front per island)
1. **Shallow-water ring** (depth 3.9, drawn first/underneath) — `generateIslandTileGrid`'s `shallowWater` pass classifies every *water* cell touching the coast (orthogonally or diagonally) by which of **its own** 4 orthogonal neighbors are land. Land on exactly 1 side → matching edge tile. Any rounded corner (land on 2 adjacent orthogonal sides, or a lone diagonal touch) → the matching corner tile alone (no fill underneath — stacking two semi-transparent tiles causes visible double-blending). Fallback (1-cell inlet/channel, 3+ sides, etc.) → plain fill. A shallow-water fill tile is also placed underneath every land corner cell (outer corners and inner-shadow corners) — the sand corner art has transparent areas in its water-facing quadrant, and without a shallow-water fill behind it the deep-water background would show through. Rendered at exact `MAP_TILE_SIZE` with **no** overlap padding — semi-transparent tiles would double-blend at seams.
2. **Sand + grass grid** (depth 4 / 4.5) — rasterizes the lobed shape onto the same `MAP_TILE_SIZE` (32px) grid, only if `sandRadius >= 75`. Every interior (`fill`) cell is grass; only the water-facing coastline ring (`edge`/`corner` cells) is sand. Each land cell picks real tile art by its 4-neighbor bitmask instead of stretching a texture — see below.
3. **Props** (depth 6) — bushes (grass islands only), rocks, occasional cannon, scattered near the coast. Rocks and bushes are generated server-side (`generateIslandProps` in `map.ts`, stored as `Obstacle.props`) so they block cannonballs; cannons are client-only decoration.
4. **Forts** (depth 5, between grass and props) — rectangular walled structures on grass islands (~50% chance). Generated server-side (`Obstacle.fortTiles`) so they block cannonballs. See `docs/mechanics/forts.md`.

## Sand/grass tile grid (`generateIslandTileGrid` in `islandShape.ts`)
- Rasterization tests each cell against the lobe union at its center **and its 4 corners** (`cellTouchesIslandShape`), not just the center point alone. The lobe union can have a narrow waist between two lobes that dips below one cell's exact center while still clearly covering part of that cell — center-only sampling turned that into a full water gap, splitting the island in two with a hard straight cut instead of an organic pinch.
- Per cell, checks which of N/S/E/W neighbors are *water*: both-N+W → `cornerTl` art, both-S+E → `cornerBr` art (and so on for the other two corners), one side only → coastline `edge` art, no water side → `fill`.
- Layer follows role, not a separate radius: `edge`/`corner` cells (touch water) are always sand — the coastline ring, exactly one tile wide. A `fill` cell (touches no water) is grass whenever the island `hasGrass` (`sandRadius >= 75`), sand otherwise. This used to be two independently-rasterized shapes — a full sand island out to `sandRadius`, plus a second, smaller grass blob out to `sandRadius * 0.6` from the same lobe union scaled down — and the two boundaries didn't reliably line up: a tighter waist in the scaled-down lobe union could pinch off a multi-cell pocket of "land but not grass" stranded deep inside the grass core with no coastline nearby to explain it, and even outside that bug the design left a whole ring of plain sand `fill` cells between the true coastline and the grass core on any wide-ringed island. Tying grass directly to the `fill` role removes both problems: there is no second radius to disagree with the first, and a grass island never shows sand `fill` tiles anywhere but its actual beach edge.
- A coastline edge/corner cell tracks `inlandIsGrass`/`diagonalIsGrass` — whether *this island has a grass core at all* (`hasGrass`) — and picks a grass-hinted art variant accordingly: `ISLAND_SAND_EDGE_GRASSTOP_KEYS`/`ISLAND_SAND_CORNER_GRASSTIP_KEYS` bake a grass hint (a strip along the top, a small fleck in the inland tip) into the *actual water-facing coastline ring*, matching the grass that now starts immediately behind it; the south-facing decorative wreck/driftwood/boulder substitutes each have a matching `_grass` variant too.
- A grass `fill` cell orthogonally next to a sand *corner* cell (`IslandGridCell.grassCornerTip`, set to the opposite corner name — e.g. next to a `cornerBl` sand corner, this cell gets `cornerTr`) renders with `ISLAND_GRASS_CORNER_KEYS` instead of plain grass fill: a corner cell's rounded sand curve, left to butt straight against a flat grass square, reads as an abrupt hard notch right where the coastline bends; the grass-corner tile (still mostly sand, grass only along its 2 far edges) tapers that same curve over one more tile before yielding to grass. A corner cell only ever has land neighbors on its 2 non-water sides, so whichever of a fill cell's 4 orthogonal neighbors turns out to be a corner cell, the opposite-corner tile is always the right pick regardless of which direction it was found in. Straight `edge` cells don't get a grass-side counterpart — only corners bend, so only corners need one.
- A sand `fill` cell (small/no-grass island only, no water on any side) still checks its 4 *diagonal* neighbors for water — but only bakes in a shadow (`IslandGridCell.innerShadowCorner` + `ISLAND_SAND_FILL_INNER_SHADOW_KEYS`) when that diagonal is a genuine re-entrant "elbow": both orthogonal neighbors bridging to that diagonal must themselves already be coastline tiles (corner/edge), not more plain fill (`isCoastline` — a first pass over the grid computes plain water-facing roles before this second pass runs, so a fill cell can check what role its neighbors got). Requiring *both* bridge cells keeps this to the one specific elbow cell it's meant for — a looser "any diagonal is water" test fires all over the interior on a small/lobed island where the coast zigzags constantly, painting what should be plain sand as one big shaded blotch (a real regression once caught).
- All island/water/fort tiles are packed into one atlas (`client/public/assets/tiles/tiles_atlas.png` + `.json`, regenerated from `sources/tiles/*.png` by `sources/pack-tiles-atlas.py`) and loaded as a single `this.load.atlas` — one HTTP request and fewer texture-swap draw-call breaks instead of ~100 individual `this.load.image` calls. The source pack mainly draws the *coastline* edge facing south (`ISLAND_SAND_EDGE_KEYS`) — north/east/west reuse that art rotated 90/180/270° (`IslandGridCell.edgeRotation`), safe because the art has no directional detail. West/north/east additionally get a little native (unrotated) art mixed in for variety — a smaller, partial water bite that's already correctly oriented (`ISLAND_SAND_EDGE_NATIVE_KEYS`; south has no such extra, only the one rotatable tile). Corners are real hand-drawn art per orientation, never rotated.
- Fill/edge/corner each have a few art variants picked at random for texture variety. **Every candidate tile was vetted pixel-by-pixel, not just at the border** — several rounds of bugs came from checking only alpha, only the border strip, or eyeballing too quickly: a colored grass corner baked into what looked like a plain sand corner; a small grass fleck baked into one of two corner art variants, inset far enough from the border that a border-only scan missed it (now `ISLAND_SAND_CORNER_GRASSTIP_KEYS`, gated on `diagonalIsGrass` instead of picked at random); 3 tiles first dismissed as a stray coastline-style stroke with no real cut, that turned out to have a genuine (if small/partial) water bite on their own side (recovered as `ISLAND_SAND_EDGE_NATIVE_KEYS`); and 4 of the 5 "plain" fill tiles turned out to each have a ~10%-area darkened quarter baked into one specific corner (tl/tr/bl/br, one per tile) — a soft shadow for the concave-diagonal-water case above, not a freely-reusable fill texture. Only 1 of the 5 was truly flat; the other 4 became `ISLAND_SAND_FILL_INNER_SHADOW_KEYS`, gated on `innerShadowCorner`. Lesson: check the whole tile's actual pixel content — alpha *and* color, the whole tile not just the border — before deciding a tile is "plain" or "unusable".
- `ISLAND_SAND_FILL_KEYS` mixes the one truly flat tile with the 2 sparkle variants at equal weight — not a rare/common split. A single identical texture repeated across a whole interior always reads as an obvious flat block once you can see many cells at once, *regardless of whether its tone matches its neighbors* (confirmed by pixel-sampling a reported "dark rectangle": it was exactly the flat tile's own slightly-darker base tone, uniformly, over every fill cell in a small island's interior — restoring 3-way variety broke up the block).
- Excluded from all of this: the pack's `fort_*` tiles (towers, walls, corners, gates — see `docs/mechanics/forts.md`), which follow the fort auto-tiling rules instead.
- Tiles render 2px oversized relative to the 32px grid spacing (`ISLAND_TILE_OVERLAP` in `obstacleView.ts`) to hide a 1px seam that otherwise appears at non-integer camera zoom.
